# THE COPY FRAMEWORK — Personal Copywriting System

> Markdown transcription of `originals/copy-framework.pdf`. If the two disagree, the PDF wins — update this file.

**Order of operations:**
1. Fill out the Product Intake (Section 1).
2. Lock the 3 core questions (Section 2).
3. Write the headline (Section 3) before anything else.
4. Draft the body following the Style Rules (Section 4).
5. Run the full checklist (Section 8) before publishing.

---

## 1. Product Intake — fill this out first
Do not write a single word of copy until every line is answered. Vague answers produce vague copy.

**Product**
- Product name
- One-sentence description
- Price / offer / guarantee
- Stated value stack (if applicable)

**Customer**
- Who exactly is the buyer? (age, situation, role)
- What keeps them up at night?
- What are they already trying to fix this problem?
- What do they secretly want? (status, relief, time, identity)
- What do they fear? (loss, embarrassment, missing out)

**Features → benefits**
- List every feature. Next to each, write the benefit it delivers.
- Rank benefits by importance to the customer (not to you).

**Proof**
- Hard facts, numbers, specs
- Reviews, testimonials, social proof
- Guarantees, certifications, awards

## 2. The Three Questions That Run Every Ad (Bob Bly)
Every piece of copy must answer these, in order. If one is missing, the copy fails.
1. Who is my customer?
2. What are the important features of the product?
3. Why will the customer want to buy it? (What feature matters most to them?)

**Core truth:** Copy cannot create desire. It only taps the hopes, dreams, fears, and desires that already exist. My job is to point existing want at this product.

## 3. The Headline — where 80% of the work lives
> "When you have written your headline, you have spent eighty cents out of your dollar." — David Ogilvy

**4 jobs of a headline:** 1) Get attention. 2) Select the audience. 3) Deliver a complete message. 4) Draw the reader into the body.

**Rules**
- Appeal to self-interest OR give news. Those are the two winners.
- Lead with a specific customer benefit, not a clever phrase.
- Cute, clever, titillating, irrelevant = dead.
- Focus on the reader's self-interest, curiosity, needs. Never the advertiser.
- Promise a reward for reading.

**Power words (Bly — use freely):** Free · How to · Why · Sale · Quick · Easy · Bargain · Last chance · Guarantee · Results · Proven · Save

**Formulas**
- How to [desired outcome] without [feared cost]
- Why [audience] are switching to [product]
- The [adjective] way to [solve specific problem]
- [Number] [things] every [audience] should know about [topic]
- Story: "They laughed when I [did X], but when [twist]…"
- News: "Finally — a [category] that [does new thing]"

**Headline test** — before approving:
- Specific benefit?
- Selects the right audience?
- A stranger knows what it's about in 3 seconds?
- Makes them want the next line?

## 4. Style Rules (non-negotiable)
**Sentences:** 14–16 words (shorter if clarity wins) · fragments · end with a preposition when natural · start with And/But/So/Because · one-sentence paragraphs as pattern interrupts · friendly, conversational.
**Words:** small words · no jargon · no sexist terms · concrete language a 10-year-old can read.
**Structure:** short sections · selling points ordered by importance · bullets to compress claims · bold/italics/CAPS/underline for emphasis.
**Tone:** respect the reader's intelligence · never talk down · give info they value · reader first, always.

## 5. Persuasion Rules
- **3-step job:** Get attention. Communicate. Persuade.
- **Relevance:** address the exact need/desire/fear that keeps them up at night.
- **Specific about the problem, vague about the solution:** name the pain in painful detail; tease the mechanism; let curiosity pull them forward; reveal the full solution only once hooked.
- **Facts sell:** numbers, dimensions, timeframes, percentages, dollar amounts. More facts, the better.
- **Modern selling points:** convenience · speed of delivery · time saving · quality at a reasonable price.
- **Fulfill the promise:** body delivers on the headline. No detours, no bait-and-switch.

## 6. Copy Structure — build order
1. **Headline** — specific benefit + curiosity hook; passes the Headline Test.
2. **Sub-headline** — expand the promise; second-strongest benefit; one sentence.
3. **Opening line** — pattern interrupt, fragment, or one startling fact.
4. **Problem** — specific, vivid; make the reader nod.
5. **Solution tease** — hint; stay vague about the mechanism.
6. **Product reveal** — name it; connect it to the problem.
7. **Features → benefits bullets.**
8. **Proof stack** — numbers, reviews, demos/before-after, guarantees.
9. **Offer** — price, value stack, bonuses, guarantee, scarcity *if real*.
10. **CTA** — specific action verb, tied to the headline. One CTA.
11. **P.S. / risk reversal** — restate the guarantee or twist the knife on the cost of not buying.

## 7. Headline Starters
- How to [outcome] in [timeframe] — even if [common objection]
- The [adjective] [product] that [does specific thing] without [pain]
- Why smart [audience] are throwing out their [old solution]
- [Number] reasons [audience] choose [product] over [competitor]
- Finally — [product] that actually [does the promised thing]
- Warning: do not buy [category] until you read this
- They laughed when I [action], but when [result]…
- The truth about [topic] that [authority] doesn't want you to know
- Save [amount/time] on [product] — guaranteed or your money back
- Free: [valuable thing] for the first [N] [audience] who [action]

## 8. Pre-Publish Checklist (every box, no exceptions)
**Customer & promise**
- [ ] Answered the 3 core questions?
- [ ] Speaks to a specific customer — not everyone?
- [ ] Taps an existing desire/fear, not an invented one?

**Headline**
- [ ] Specific benefit?
- [ ] Passes the 4 Headline Jobs?
- [ ] No cute, clever, or irrelevant phrasing?

**Style**
- [ ] Sentences mostly 14–16 words?
- [ ] Fragments, conjunction starts, one-sentence paragraphs?
- [ ] Bullets where lists compress claims?
- [ ] Bold / italics / caps on key phrases?
- [ ] Zero jargon? Small words?

**Persuasion**
- [ ] Specific about problem, vague about mechanism?
- [ ] More facts than fluff?
- [ ] Speed, convenience, time-saving called out?
- [ ] Body delivers on the headline?

**Offer & CTA**
- [ ] Price, value stack, guarantee visible?
- [ ] One clear CTA, action verb, tied to headline?
- [ ] P.S. or risk reversal at the end?

## 9. Core Principles
- Copy cannot create desire. It focuses existing desires onto a particular product.
- The headline is the telegram that decides whether the copy gets read.
- The best headlines appeal to self-interest, or give news.
- Get attention. Communicate. Persuade.
- Put the reader first.
- **The job:** study the product, find what's unique, present that uniqueness so the consumer is motivated to buy.
