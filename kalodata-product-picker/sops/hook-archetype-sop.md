# HOOK ARCHETYPE SOP — Product → Viral Hook Classifier

> Geecommerce Solutions | Internal SOP v1.0. Derived from scene-level analysis of 10 viral TikTok Shop ads (July 2026 wave).
> Markdown transcription of `originals/hook-archetype-sop.pdf`. If the two disagree, the PDF wins — update this file.

## How to use
1. Know the two mechanics and four subjects (Section 1). That is the whole classification system.
2. Classify the product (Section 5 prompt logic).
3. Use the matching archetype card (Section 3) as the shot-by-shot brief for Higgsfield prompts.
4. **Kill the product if it fails Section 2. Don't skip this.** A great hook on a Tier-2 product just buys you chargebacks faster.

---

## Section 1 — Classification system

### Variable A — The Mechanic
| Mechanic | Definition | Corpus examples |
|---|---|---|
| **EXTRACTION** | Pulls something disgusting OUT and shows it on the tool | Crevice brush (lint clump), dematting rake (undercoat sheets), turmeric pads (blackheads) |
| **TRANSFORMATION** | Converts chaos → order in one continuous motion | Trimmer (overgrown→clean rows), spray comb (frizz→sleek), tablets (filth→gleaming) |

**Extraction is the stronger mechanic.** Grosser, more rewatchable, and it can't be faked into a claim gap — the filth on the brush is real. Transformation is easier to shoot but easier to over-promise. When both are available, lead extraction.

### Variable B — The Subject
| Subject | Acts on | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| PERSON | Hair, skin, body | Public ambush hooks (3, 4, 5) |
| OBJECT/SURFACE | Shoes, floors, blinds, vents, car | Incumbent-tool hooks (1, 2, 8) |
| ANIMAL | Pets, livestock | Distress hooks (6) + absurd montage escalation |
| ENVIRONMENT | Yard, garage, infestation | Chaos hooks (10) |

### Variable C — The Three Enablers (YES/NO each; they gate which archetypes are shootable)
- **MOCKABLE INCUMBENT** — a visible, stupid-looking old tool it replaces? (broom, scissors, flat iron, push mower)
- **PORTABLE + INSTANT** — pulled from a pocket, visible payoff in under 5 seconds?
- **DISTRESS CARRIER** — a third party who visibly suffers from the problem? (crying kid, growling dog, frustrated spouse)

## Section 2 — The Kill Gate (run before any creative work)
| Gate | Question | Fail = |
|---|---|---|
| **G1 — Payload** | Visible payload (extraction or transformation)? | No payload, no ad. **Kill.** |
| **G2 — Zero explanation** | Can a stranger understand it with the sound off in 3 seconds? | Needs a paragraph → **Kill.** |
| **G3 — Claim honesty** | Is the AI demo an *amplification* of what it really does, or a *fabrication*? | Fabrication → **Kill.** |
| **G4 — Price** | Lands under $25 retail with 3x+ margin? | No → **Kill.** |
| **G5 — Incumbent** | Does it make an existing tool look stupid? | No → weak, proceed with caution. |

**G3 is the one that costs money.** Crevice brush, dematting rake, trimmer and sneaker cleaner all pass — exaggerated but real. The collagen hair mask "straightening an afro in one comb stroke" and pest pucks "gone overnight" fail. Claim gaps drive refund rate, dispute rate, processor risk, and shop suspension. Corpus items are format references only — never product references.

## Section 3 — The Ten Archetypes

### Universal skeleton (the actual finding of the corpus)
```
HOOK (12–30% of runtime)   — two people, one interrupts the other, physical escalation
                           — DIEGETIC AUDIO (real dialogue, sounds like a caught moment)
MONEY SHOT (15–30%)        — one continuous motion, NO CUT during payoff
WITNESS BEAT (5–10%)       — bystander reacts inside the frame
MONTAGE (40–55%)           — 3–7 contexts, ~3s each
                           — VOICEOVER AUDIO (switches here; now it sounds like an ad)
CTA (5–8%)                 — scarcity + price
```
- **Target runtime 30–40s.** Corpus range 21–49s, avg 36s. Does NOT work at 15s — the montage IS the proof.
- **Audio switch** is the most-missed mechanic. Hook = character dialogue + ambient sound. Body = clean voiceover. Female VO for beauty/person subjects; male VO for tools/surfaces.

### 1 — The Underdog Upset
- **Source:** Cordless grass trimmer (88M views / 6 days)
- **Psychology:** "So easy that *she* beat *him*." If the least-expected person wins, the product is the only explanation.
- **Casting:** Underdog (elderly woman, small teen) vs. expected winner (large/fit man) with the incumbent tool.
- **Beats:** staged competition (balloon arch, start line, crowd) → countdown → underdog cruises, expected winner struggles (drags, sweats, trips) → underdog wins, crowd erupts → money shot: ASMR close-ups of clean result → montage → CTA.
- **Best fit:** OBJECT/ENVIRONMENT + mockable incumbent + physical-effort problem.
- **Disqualifier:** Anything that can't be "raced." No timed outcome = no hook.

### 2 — The Public Showdown
- **Source:** Electric spray comb
- **Psychology:** Peer validation at scale. Twenty phones recording = "this is already viral." Manufactured bandwagon.
- **Casting:** Pro with legacy tool vs. amateur with product. Crowd of 15–25 filming on phones.
- **Beats:** "Three, two, one, GO!" → pro works legacy tool → amateur makes 2 passes, instant absurd result → subject stands, spins, shows it off → crowd covers mouths, pro clutches face → signature line "The person who invented this deserves a Nobel Prize" → feature stack → montage → CTA.
- **Best fit:** PERSON + multi-feature device + existing pro/salon alternative.
- **Disqualifier:** Slow products. Needs an instant result under crowd scrutiny.

### 3 — The Stranger's Hands
- **Source:** Hair oil (World Cup stadium)
- **Psychology:** Personal-space violation = involuntary attention. The victim's "Stop!" is the viewer's own reaction.
- **Casting:** Unsuspecting subject in a crowded public place; anonymous hand enters frame; bystanders visible.
- **Beats:** subject with visible problem (2s max) → stranger's hand enters with product → "What are you doing? Stop!" → money shot: ONE continuous motion held 8–9s, no cut → crowd amazed → 2–3 more subjects/settings, same motion → CTA.
- **Best fit:** PERSON + portable + instant + visible-in-public problem.
- **Disqualifier:** Anything needing setup, water, or dwell time.
- **TREND-STACK SLOT:** the setting is a skin. Rotate it to whatever is culturally loudest that week — a prompt change, not a reshoot.

### 4 — The POV Ambush
- **Source:** Turmeric & kojic pads (restaurant)
- **Psychology:** First-person camera makes the viewer the perpetrator. Plus comedic product absurdity (foam eruption) reads as entertainment.
- **Casting:** POV hand (you). Target seated in upscale/formal setting. 4+ background patrons.
- **Beats:** POV hand holds product, target unaware (1s) → product slapped on: "Oh! No way! Whoa!" → absurd spectacle (foam erupts/expands) → wipe-off reveal → patrons "Ooh! Ah!" → at-home benefit stack + texture ASMR → CTA.
- **Best fit:** PERSON + consumable + inherently weird visual behavior (foam, fizz, color change).
- **Disqualifier:** Boring-looking products. The product itself must be a spectacle.

### 5 — The Slap
- **Source:** Sneaker cleaner (ballroom)
- **Psychology:** Highest-escalation hook in the corpus. The injustice is the retention driver.
- **Casting:** Helper (waiter/staff) + victim in formal wear with visible catastrophe (wine stain). Formal crowd.
- **Beats:** helper sprays the stain on her back → wipe, gone → she spins "What are you doing?!" — SLAPS him, crowd gasps, he smiles → he points at a second stain on the front → spray, wipe, gone: "Oh my god! It looks brand new!" → hard cut to gloved-hand tutorial montage → CTA.
- **Best fit:** OBJECT (worn/carried) + instant + emergency-grade visible disaster.
- **Disqualifier:** Chronic rather than acute "disasters." Needs a stain-level catastrophe.

### 6 — The Danger Rescue
- **Source:** Pet dematting rake
- **Psychology:** "You're doing it wrong AND you're hurting them." Guilt + fear. "That's why he growled" reframes the viewer's whole history with the problem.
- **Casting:** Amateur doing it dangerously + expert (groomer in scrubs) + distressed party (animal, child).
- **Beats:** amateur uses dangerous incumbent (scissors on a mat) → animal growls/lunges, amateur recoils (2s) → expert kneels: "Loosen it first." / "That much?" / "That's why he growled." → money shot: undercoat sheets off onto the ground → ESCALATING ABSURDITY MONTAGE: dog → long-haired dog → sheep → horse → camel → pony → cow.
- **Best fit:** ANIMAL + extraction + credible injury risk from the old method.
- **Disqualifier:** No distress carrier = no hook.
- The escalating montage is the competitive weapon — camel and cow are unfilmable for real UGC competitors.

### 7 — The Crying Child Rescue
- **Source:** Collagen hair mask *(format reference only — product fails G3)*
- **Psychology:** Child distress is the highest-priority interrupt in the brain. Parent-guilt targeting.
- **Casting:** Struggling parent (dad reads as out of his depth) + crying child + rescuer.
- **Beats:** child screaming, parent failing with incumbent: "This is too hard!" → "These knots are too tight." → rescuer enters with product → money shot: one application, one stroke, child stops crying → VO: "This solves [problem] easily" → montage of 3 more people → CTA.
- **Best fit:** PERSON/ANIMAL + child-adjacent + parent-guilt category.
- **Disqualifier:** Adult-only products. **Run G3 hard** — this hook tempts fabricated claims.

### 8 — The Intervention
- **Source:** Crevice cleaning brush
- **Psychology:** Domestic authority. Lowest production cost, highest trust — reads as a real household.
- **Casting:** Partner A doing it the hard way + Partner B walking in with the product. Home setting.
- **Beats:** Partner A on the floor with pathetic incumbent (broom on a door track) → Partner B: **"Drop that right now."** (2s) → **MONEY SHOT HELD 13 SECONDS** — scrub, pull out a massive disgusting clump, A watches wide-eyed in frame → SURFACE AVALANCHE: tower fan → blinds → pet crate → ceiling fan → fridge top → air vent → baseboards (~3s each) → tool loaded with filth, one tap on trash rim, self-cleans → CTA: "Tap the link below before it sells out."
- **Best fit:** OBJECT/SURFACE + extraction + many surfaces + mockable incumbent.
- **Disqualifier:** Single-use-case products. Lives or dies on surface count.
- **The cleanest template in the corpus.** Real capability, gross-satisfying extraction, infinite montage, zero claim-gap exposure. **If a product fits this and another archetype, default here.**

### 9 — The Costume Interrupt
- **Source:** Cleaning tablets (man in a pink tutu)
- **Psychology:** Pure pattern interrupt; the absurdity is unrelated to the product. **Universal fallback** for products with no natural drama.
- **Casting:** Someone visually incongruous with the task (muscular man in pink ruffled blouse + tutu, barefoot) + straight-man witness (woman with baby in carrier).
- **Beats:** absurd person mid-task, filthy environment — hold 14s → "Try this." / "What is this?" → money shot inside the hook: drop tablet, water turns vibrant blue, mop leaves a gleaming path → witness: "Whoo! Oh my god, it's amazing!" → montage: algae deck → kitchen floor → spray bottle → greasy backsplash → marker-covered mirror → CTA.
- **Best fit:** ANY subject. **Disqualifier:** none — but it's the weakest archetype when a better fit exists; it borrows attention rather than earning it.

### 10 — The Infestation Panic
- **Source:** Pest repellent pucks *(format reference only — product fails G3)*
- **Psychology:** Environmental chaos + a comically wrong solution. Disgust travels further than desire.
- **Casting:** Panicking group + one person with an absurd wrong tool (salt gun at rats) + calm authority with product.
- **Beats:** environment overrun mid-event (rats at a BBQ), plates dropping (2s) → wrong-tool comedy, camera shake, "What the—" → calm person: **"Stop doing that."** places product → pests flee → problem montage: garden beds → raccoons in trash → chipmunks at pet bowls → shed gaps → testimonial pivot: "I'm honestly so tired of this" → CTA: "Grab yours while the deal is still live."
- **Best fit:** ENVIRONMENT + removal/repellent + recurring invasion problem.
- **Disqualifier:** **Run G3 at maximum severity.** Pest control carries FTC/EPA claim exposure. Only for a product that genuinely works.

## Section 4 — Decision Matrix
| If the product… | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| PERSON + portable + instant | 3 Stranger's Hands | 4 POV Ambush |
| PERSON + weird visual behavior (foam/fizz) | 4 POV Ambush | 3 Stranger's Hands |
| PERSON + replaces a salon/pro service | 2 Public Showdown | 1 Underdog Upset |
| OBJECT + many surfaces + extraction | **8 The Intervention** | 1 Underdog Upset |
| OBJECT + acute visible disaster (stains) | 5 The Slap | 8 The Intervention |
| OBJECT/ENVIRONMENT + physical effort + timeable | 1 Underdog Upset | 2 Public Showdown |
| ANIMAL + extraction + injury risk from old way | 6 Danger Rescue | 8 The Intervention |
| ANIMAL/PERSON + child-adjacent | 7 Crying Child Rescue | 6 Danger Rescue |
| ENVIRONMENT + invasion/recurring | 10 Infestation Panic | 9 Costume Interrupt |
| No incumbent, no distress carrier, no public use | 9 Costume Interrupt | 8 The Intervention |

## Section 5 — Classifier output format
```
KILL GATE: [pass/fail per gate, one line each]
MECHANIC: [extraction/transformation + one line why]
SUBJECT: [person/object/animal/environment]
ENABLERS: [incumbent Y/N, portable+instant Y/N, distress carrier Y/N]
SURFACE COUNT: [number + list them]

RANK 1 — ARCHETYPE #[n] [NAME] — confidence [high/med/low]
  Why it fits: [2 lines]
  The cast: [who's in it]
  The hook line: [actual line of dialogue, in quotes]
  The money shot: [what motion, how long, what's the visible payload]
  The montage: [contexts in escalating order]

RANK 2 — ARCHETYPE #[n] [NAME] — confidence [high/med/low]
  [same fields, condensed]

RANK 3 — ARCHETYPE #[n] [NAME] — confidence [high/med/low]
  [one line each]

DO NOT USE: [which archetypes are wrong for this product and why]
BIGGEST RISK: [one line — the thing most likely to kill this]
```
If G1, G2, G3 or G4 fails: output `KILL — [gate], [reason]` and STOP.

### Rules
- Target runtime 30–40s. NOT 15s.
- Universal skeleton: HOOK (12–30%) → MONEY SHOT (15–30%, no cut) → WITNESS BEAT (5–10%) → MONTAGE (40–55%, 3–7 contexts @ ~3s) → CTA (5–8%, scarcity + price).
- AUDIO SWITCH: hook = diegetic dialogue + ambient; body = clean VO. Female VO for person subjects, male VO for tools/surfaces.
- Hook must be TWO PEOPLE, one interrupting the other, with PHYSICAL escalation. Never a talking head. Never product-first.
- The old way must be shown FAILING, in-frame, before the product appears.
- **Be blunt.** If the product is weak, say it's weak. Do not manufacture enthusiasm.

## Section 6 — What this system actually is
- **The hook has nothing to do with the product.** A slap, a growl, a race, a tutu — attention devices bolted onto anything with a visible payload.
- **The hook library is the asset, not the product.** Build the ten as re-skinnable Higgsfield scene prompts once, then rotate products through them.

### Standing rules
- **Saturation (as of July 2026):** hair is dead (3/10 of corpus), cleaning is crowded (3/10). Yard and pet are the only un-flooded categories. → see `rubric/saturation.md` for the live list.
- **Trend-stack slot:** Archetype 3's setting is a skin. Rotate weekly; generate and post within 48 hours of the moment.
- **Kill rule:** 10–14 days, no exceptions.
- **Metric:** shares and DM-sends. Not likes.
